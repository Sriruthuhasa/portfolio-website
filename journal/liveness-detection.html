Building Liveness Detection: Why Screenshots Still Fool Most Systems

When building the facial authentication system for my Chrome Extension project, I quickly learned that "face detection" and "liveness detection" are two very different problems. Most open-source face recognition libraries will happily authenticate a printed photo held up to a webcam. That's a problem.

What is a Presentation Attack?

A presentation attack is when someone tries to spoof a biometric system using a non-live representation: a photo, a looping video, a 3D mask. The goal of liveness detection — also called Presentation Attack Detection (PAD) — is to distinguish a real, live face from these artefacts.

The Three Layers We Implemented

1. Texture Analysis

Real faces have micro-textures: skin pores, subtle hair, blemish variation. Printed photos and screens flatten these. We used Local Binary Pattern (LBP) features to detect this flattening — screens in particular have a grid-like moiré pattern at close range that LBP catches reliably.

2. Motion-Based Challenges

We added randomized challenges: blink detection, slight head turn prompts, and smile detection using facial landmark tracking. A looping video attack fails these because the video was recorded before the challenge was issued. This is called challenge-response liveness detection.

3. Depth Estimation (experimental)

Using optical flow between video frames, we estimated whether the face had genuine 3D depth. A flat photo moves uniformly when the user tilts their head; a real face creates differential motion across its planes. This was the hardest to get right without a depth sensor, but even a rough estimate reduced false positives significantly.

What We Didn't Solve

High-quality 3D masks remain a hard problem without depth sensors or IR cameras. Our software-only approach handles 95%+ of real-world attack scenarios, but it's not a substitute for hardware-level liveness detection in high-security environments. The goal for a Chrome Extension was pragmatic security — not defeating nation-state adversaries.

The takeaway: liveness detection is a layered problem. No single technique is sufficient. Combining texture analysis, motion challenges, and depth cues gives you a system that's genuinely hard to spoof without specialized hardware.
